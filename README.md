# Coevolution

**Science Production and Absorption in Corporate America, 1945-1980**

Why do some firms produce scientific research while others draw heavily on discoveries made elsewhere?

## About the research

This project studies how publicly traded U.S. firms produce science and turn it into invention. It links corporate publications, patents, citations from patents to scientific papers, and historical records of laboratories and scientific personnel.

The study distinguishes firms that lead scientific production from firms that use external science intensively while publishing little themselves. It examines how these strategies relate to scientific staffing, connections between researchers and inventors, and the use of a firm's own discoveries.

The central finding is that producing science and applying it are distinct organizational activities. Firms can draw heavily on outside discoveries without maintaining large research programs, while producing firms tend to cite their own discoveries earlier. These are descriptive findings about the organization of corporate research and invention.
